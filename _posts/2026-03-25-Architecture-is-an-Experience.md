Architects and interior designers often describe the same frustrating loop: a drawing is presented, the client nods, and weeks later the client says "this is not what I imagined". The drawing was accurate. The client simply could not read it.

## Why approvals drag

- Clients picture rooms larger or smaller than the plan suggests.
- They cannot judge ceiling heights, door positions or storage from a 2D drawing.
- Finishes look different on a screen, in daylight and under evening light.
- Family members who were not in the meeting raise new questions later.

Each misunderstanding found after construction starts costs real money. Found at design stage it costs a conversation.

## Where a walkthrough fits in the design process

1. **Concept stage.** A rough, white-box 3D model to test massing, flow and proportions with the client.
2. **Design development.** Add furniture layouts, openings and ceiling heights. Let the client walk it and mark what feels wrong.
3. **Finish selection.** Show two or three material and colour schemes in the same space and let the client toggle between them.
4. **Pre-construction sign-off.** A final walkthrough link the client shares with family, which becomes the reference for handover.

## How to run the review meeting

- Send the link before the meeting so the client arrives having explored.
- Ask them to find three things they like and three they would change.
- Compare options inside the same model so the decision is visual and direct.
- Record the agreed version number so changes later are easy to discuss.

## Business benefits beyond approvals

- **Fewer revision loops.** Decisions are made on what the client has seen.
- **Stronger proposals.** A walkable proposal stands out from a PDF of renders when you are competing for a project.
- **Referrals.** A client who shows the walkthrough to friends is advertising your practice.
- **Developer relationships.** Developers value consultants who help them sell, not only design.

## What to look for in a 3D partner

- Models built to the true dimensions of your drawings, with a stated tolerance.
- The ability to update the model when the design changes.
- A browser link the client can open on any phone.
- Control over lighting and materials, so renders match what will be built.
- A process that fits your timeline, including quick turnarounds for client meetings.

## Pricing a walkthrough as a design phase

- Offer it as a defined phase (for example 'visual design review') with a fixed fee, a delivery date and a number of revision rounds.
- Show how it removes change orders: one avoided change often pays for the phase.
- Include a developer-facing version if the project will be sold off-plan.
