Nobody should throw away their brochure. The question is what to lead with when a buyer messages you at 10 pm on a Tuesday.

## Four formats, compared honestly

| | PDF brochure | Walkthrough video | Interactive 3D walkthrough | Physical sample flat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Best at** | Facts, specifications, legal details | Emotion and social ads | Understanding layout, light and scale | Material feel and scale in person |
| **Weak at** | Showing how a home feels | Letting the buyer choose where to look | Replacing the feel of real materials | Reaching anyone who is not in the room |
| **Buyer control** | Scrolls | Watches | Walks anywhere | Walks anywhere |
| **Sharing** | Easy (file) | Easy (link) | Easy (link) | Not shareable |
| **Cost to change** | Low | High (re-render) | Moderate (update the model) | High |
| **Reach** | Anyone | Anyone | Anyone | One location |

## Why a PDF is not enough on its own

A brochure is built for the printer, then adapted for the screen. On a phone it often means pinching and zooming through a heavy file. It is excellent for specifications, floor plan dimensions and legal information, and you should keep it. It is poor at answering the questions buyers actually ask: How does this room feel? What will I see? Is it better than the other project?

## Why video is not enough either

Video is a strong tool for attention. But a buyer shortlisting a purchase wants to look at the balcony, not the part the director chose to show. Video also becomes expensive to change when the plan or finishes are updated.

## The hybrid that works

1. **Lead with the 3D link.** One tap, any phone.
2. **Follow with a short PDF** for specifications, pricing, payment plan and statutory details.
3. **Use stills and a short reel** in ads and social media to drive people to the link.
4. **Invite them to a sample flat or site visit** with their questions ready.

## A note on compliance

In Maharashtra, project advertisements need the MahaRERA registration number and QR code, and in Dubai, the permit number. Whichever format you use, keep those visible. For a 3D link, place them in the viewer footer and on the landing page.

## What about a physical sample flat?

It is still valuable for material feel and for buyers who want to touch finishes. But it costs fit-out, space, upkeep and staff, and it serves only people who can visit. Many developers now use a digital sample flat to pre-qualify visitors, so the physical flat is used for closing instead of first impressions. We break down the costs in a separate guide.

## Quick audit

Open your last three buyer messages. What did you send? If it was only a PDF, test sending a link alongside it for a month and compare reply rates and site visits.

## What goes in each asset

| Asset | Contains | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1-page fact sheet | Location, carpet areas, price range, payment plan, possession date, registration details | Verify |
| 3D link | Walkable unit types, sun, views | Understand and feel |
| Hero stills | 4 to 6 images | Social and ads |
| Reel | 15 to 30 seconds | Attention |
| Full brochure | Everything else | Reference |
