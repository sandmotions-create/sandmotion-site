**Key takeaways**

- Visualization is a cost until it changes a number you can measure: speed to sell, cost per visit, bookings from wider reach, or negotiation pressure.
- Carrying cost of unsold stock is often the biggest lever: each month faster saves interest and overheads.
- Use conservative estimates, test on one project and measure.
- Do not expect price premiums: the independent evidence on price is mixed.

## The four levers

1. **Time:** every month faster to sell out saves carrying costs (interest, overheads, sales team).
2. **Efficiency:** fewer wasted site visits and shorter sales conversations lower cost per booking.
3. **Reach:** buyers who cannot visit (NRIs, overseas investors) add bookings.
4. **Negotiation:** clearer products can reduce discount requests. Treat this as a hypothesis to measure, not a promise.

## What the evidence does and does not say

A UK study reported higher conversion and sale prices for guided virtual viewings, while a Harvard Business School study of 75,000 home sales found no significant price effect but better screening. So build your ROI case on speed, efficiency and reach, and treat price uplift as upside you have measured, not assumed.

## How to measure each lever

| Efficiency | Cost per site visit and per booking | | |

## A cautious ROI example (illustrative)

If your unsold inventory costs ₹15 lakh a month to carry and clearer presentation helps you sell out one month sooner, you save ₹15 lakh. If two extra bookings come from wider reach at ₹5 lakh net margin each, add ₹10 lakh. Against a ₹3 lakh investment, the benefit is large, but only if the assumptions hold. That is why you test.
